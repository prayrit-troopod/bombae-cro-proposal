Bombae shoppers want to buy. The journey keeps getting in their way.

This proposal turns the findings from our Bombae CRO baseline into a CRO programme for mybombae.in. It covers only the barriers a shopper hits on the way from landing to placing an order. Every product page runs on the same theme, cart and checkout, so we show each barrier on one worked example: the Fuzzoff 6-in-1 bikini trimmer page, Bombae's second hero product. All screenshots were taken on the live site on a 375px phone screen on 16 Oct 2026.

The barriers, group by group

What we saw, why it costs orders, and what we will change

Each barrier applies to the whole store. The screenshots show it on the example page, the Fuzzoff 6-in-1 PDP, and the text says where else it shows up. P0 means fix or test in the first sprint. P1 follows once the buy path is unblocked. Tap a barrier to open it.

01

Unblock the path to purchase

She has already decided to buy. The site then makes it hard to pay.

Every shopper here has chosen the product, so each fix wins back an order that was already ours. That makes these the fastest, cheapest gains, and the place to start.

  • B1 Add to Cart leads nowhere
  • B2 Checkout drop-off
  • B3 No buy button for returning carts
  • B4 Cart drawer distracts
B1P0Example: Fuzzoff PDP + Clarity (Hairess data)

Add to Cart leads nowhere: no cart opens and no Checkout button appears
What we saw on the example page
  • After tapping Add to Cart, a grey "Product added to cart" toast shows for about 2 seconds over the title. It can't be tapped.
  • Both Add to Cart buttons turn into a "− 1 +" stepper. No cart drawer opens and no Checkout button appears.
  • The round scroll-to-top button sits on top of the sticky stepper's "+".
Why it costs orders

To continue, the shopper has to find the small bag icon at the top right. On Hairess, about 7,700 Add to Cart taps led to only 507 taps on the cart icon. Store-wide, 45% of carts never start checkout. On Fuzzoff, 55% never do. The GoKwik drawer with a Checkout button already works. It just doesn't open after Add to Cart.

What we will change
  • Open the GoKwik cart drawer on Add to Cart, with Checkout in view.
  • A/B test this against a "Buy Now" button that goes straight to checkout.
  • Hide the scroll-to-top button while the sticky bar is visible.

Primary metric: cart → checkout (54.6% store-wide)

B2P0GA4 + Shopify

About 3 in 4 started checkouts don't become orders, and we can't see why yet
What we saw

Store-wide, Shopify's 9,537 orders are about 24% of the 39,286 checkouts GA4 records. On the example page, 6,763 Fuzzoff checkouts led to 1,896 purchases in GA4 (28.0%). Every step from phone number and OTP to address and payment runs inside GoKwik, and none of that reaches GA4 in a usable form.

Why it costs orders

This is the largest drop in the journey. Raising store checkout completion from 24% to 30% would mean about 2,250 more orders a month.

What we will change
  • Pull the GoKwik step funnel (phone → OTP → address → payment → order) and the split by payment method.
  • Test a prepaid offer shown before the payment step, and make any COD fee clear.
  • Show the free gift as a line in the order summary.
Needed first: GoKwik access

We need access to the GoKwik merchant dashboard before we can recommend a credible fix for checkout. Without the step funnel, payment-method split and drop-off reasons, we can only guess where the 3 in 4 shoppers leave. The tests above are starting hypotheses, and we will confirm or replace them once the GoKwik data is in.

Primary metric: checkout → order (~24% store-wide)

B3P0Example: Fuzzoff PDP

Returning shoppers with the item already in their cart see no buy button at all
What we saw on the example page

We reloaded the example page with Fuzzoff already in the cart. The baseline audit found the same on Hairess. Every buy button, in the page and in the sticky bar, showed only "− 1 +". There was no Add to Cart, Buy Now or Checkout anywhere.

Why it costs orders

Shoppers coming back from a cart-abandonment ad or WhatsApp reminder are the most likely to buy. They land on a page with nothing to tap that moves them forward.

What we will change

When the item is already in the cart, show "Checkout · [price]" beside the quantity on every PDP, in the page and in the sticky bar.

Primary metric: checkout starts from retargeting traffic

B4P1Example: Fuzzoff PDP

The cart drawer pushes shoppers to hunt for coupons and hides the next reward
What we saw on the example page
  • The drawer says "You have unlocked all rewards!" at ₹1,199 (and at ₹1,799 on Hairess). Meanwhile, the announcement bar rotates "Free Tote Bag on order above ₹2,500".
  • An open "Enter coupon code" field and "View all offers (8)" sit above everything else.
  • The add-on cards under "Offers Unlocked!" are cut off, so the product names can't be read.
  • The prepaid incentive is ₹20 off UPI.
Why it costs orders

A coupon field at the top sends shoppers off to search for codes, and some never come back. Telling shoppers they have "all rewards" removes the reason to add one more item. On Hairess, the drawer cross-sells body wax strips instead of the ₹399 Heat Protectant.

What we will change
  • Collapse the coupon field behind an "Have a code?" link.
  • Show a progress bar to the next reward, for example "₹801 away from a free pouch".
  • Show one readable add-on card that matches the product in the cart (Soothing Spray with Fuzzoff, Heat Protectant with Hairess). Test a larger prepaid incentive.

Primary metric: cart → checkout · AOV · prepaid share

02

Remove the doubts at the moment of decision

She wants it, but is checking whether she can afford it and trust it.

These shoppers are one answer away from Add to Cart. The page either has no answer, or gives two that contradict each other.

  • B5 EMI screen has no way to buy
  • B6 Mixed warranty and delivery messages
B5P0Example: Fuzzoff PDP + Clarity (Hairess data)

"Buy on EMI" opens a screen with no way to buy, and dates that are months out of date
What we saw on the example page
  • Tapping "Buy on EMI" opens "Pay only ₹400 Now". The only button on that screen is the close icon.
  • The instalments are dated "3rd May" and "3rd Jun". It is October.
  • The last line, "Select Bombae Pay Later on the payment screen", is plain text and can't be tapped.
23%of all taps on Hairess go to EMI
5,535modal opens
5,291closes
Why it costs orders

The same modal runs on both hero PDPs. Tapping EMI is the strongest buying signal on the page. These shoppers want the product and are checking whether they can afford it. When they decide they can, the page sends them back to the start.

What we will change
  • Add a "Buy on EMI · Pay ₹400 today" button inside the modal (₹600 on Hairess). One tap adds the item to the cart and opens GoKwik checkout with Bombae Pay Later already selected.
  • Generate the instalment dates from today's date.
  • Show "or ₹400 × 3 at 0% interest" in line with the price, on every PDP.

Primary metric: EMI modal → checkout rate · PDP conversion

B6P1Example: Fuzzoff PDP

Mixed messages on warranty and delivery chip away at trust
What we saw on the example page
  • The bullets and the "Best bits" badge say 1-year warranty. The text under the sticky Add to Cart says 1.5-Years Exclusive Warranty. Both are visible on the same screen.
  • The delivery box reads "Delivery estimate unavailable" until the shopper types a pincode. No delivery date shows by default.
Why it costs orders

Hairess has the same problem: "2-year warranty" in the bullets, "1.5-Years" at the sticky button, and an announcement bar that says "Best Selling Women's Trimmer in India" on a hair styler. Two warranty numbers make shoppers doubt both. On Hairess, pincode checks were 7.8% of all taps, so delivery timing clearly matters to buyers.

What we will change
  • Use one warranty number per product everywhere, and make the announcement bar match the product.
  • Add a trust row under the price on every PDP: warranty · easy returns · "Delivery by [date]", estimated from the visitor's location.

Primary metric: add-to-cart rate · pincode check usage

03

Put the proof where shoppers actually look

She is still deciding, and the evidence that would convince her is where she never scrolls.

Once the buy path works, more shoppers need to reach it. This group moves reviews, answers and results into the first screen, where most visitors make up their minds.

  • B7 Reviews sit too low
  • B8 First-time questions hidden
  • B9 First screen has no proof
B7P0Example: Fuzzoff PDP + Clarity (Hairess data)

Reviews and customer photos sit below the point where most shoppers stop
What we saw on the example page
  • Ratings and customer photos start at 64% of the page, after the FAQ. Written reviews start at 71%.
  • The customer photos show only unboxings. None of them show a result.
  • "Best bits" and the feature banners take up 26–49% of the page and repeat what the bullets already say.
Why it costs orders

On Hairess, reviews start at 62% of the page and only about 6% of visitors scroll that far. Those who do give them 6.5× more attention than the hero image. On every PDP, the proof that sells is in the section almost nobody reaches.

What we will change
  • Put a review snippet carousel directly under Add to Cart on every PDP, led by reviews that answer the product's main doubt ("no cuts" for Fuzzoff, "works on curly hair" for Hairess).
  • Cut the banner block to 2 visuals and move the video feed and reviews above it.
  • Ask buyers for result photos and videos in post-purchase review requests.

Primary metric: add-to-cart rate · share of visitors who see a review

B8P1Example: Fuzzoff PDP + competitor review

First-time buyer questions are hidden in closed accordions halfway down
What we saw on the example page

"I'm new to trimming, how to get started?", "Does the trimmer have any side effects?" and "Where does this trimmer work?" are the questions a first-time buyer has. They sit at 49% of the page, all closed, under the heading "for the truth seekers".

Why it costs orders

On Hairess, 2,920 taps went to the same accordion, so shoppers do look for these answers. Hairess buyers have their own first-timer questions ("will it straighten curly hair?", "will it fry my hair?"), and the page doesn't answer them either. Competitors answer them upfront: Sirona with how-to guides by body area, Alan Truman with curly-hair guidance.

What we will change
  • Add a suitability chip row near the price, with each chip linking to a 10-second how-to clip. On Fuzzoff: "Where it works" (brows, face, underarms, bikini, legs). On Hairess: "Works on: straight · wavy · curly · thick".
  • Open the top two first-timer answers by default and move them above the banners.
  • Add a one-line cost comparison against repeat salon visits, using real local salon prices.

Primary metric: add-to-cart rate for new visitors · FAQ opens

B9P0Example: Fuzzoff PDP + Clarity (Hairess data)

The first screen is a pack shot and a block of text, with no result and no proof
What we saw on the example page
  • The first screen shows a pack shot, the title, "100% painfree trim", the price and the EMI box. The claim has nothing on screen to back it up.
  • Under the EMI box, four emoji bullet points in running text carry the main selling points.
  • The rating (4.73, 400 reviews) is plain text. None of the customer photos appear here.
Why it costs orders

On Hairess, 46% of all attention goes to the top 10% of the page, and most visitors decide from that screen alone. On Fuzzoff the first question is "will it cut me?" On Hairess it is "will it work on my hair?" Neither first screen answers it.

What we will change
  • Make gallery slide 2 on every hero PDP a 6-second result loop: a smooth, no-nick trim for Fuzzoff, a before and after on real hair for Hairess.
  • Replace the emoji bullets with 4 icon USPs (on Fuzzoff: bikini-safe, waterproof, 6 heads, warranty).
  • Put a strip of review photos and "400 reviews" next to the stars, with a link to the reviews.

Primary metric: view → add to cart (12.2%) · reach at 20% depth

Make the pages load faster

Add-on, separately charged.

A slow page loses shoppers before any of the above can help. It lifts every group, but it is a separate technical workstream.

  • B10 Pages are heavy
B10P0TrooPerformance audit · Lighthouse 13.5, mobile · 16 Oct 2026

Product pages are heavy, so many shoppers leave before they finish loading
What we saw on the example page
  • The Fuzzoff 6-in-1 page scores 37/100 on Google PageSpeed for mobile.
  • The main product image reaches the browser in half a second, then waits another 1.9 seconds behind scripts before it appears.
  • The page ships 2.5 MB of JavaScript, and over 1 MB of it is never used. Render-blocking files hold back the first paint by 2.1 seconds.
  • The phone's main thread is busy for 12.9 seconds.
  • For real shoppers over the last 28 days, the page fails Core Web Vitals. The main image takes 3s to show (Google's limit is 2.5s), and a tap takes 214ms to respond (the limit is 200ms).

Most of the weight comes from third-party apps that load on every page: SagePilot (671 KiB), GoKwik KwikCart and KwikPass (another 800 KiB to 1 MB), the Meta pixel, Google Tag Manager, Microsoft Clarity, Google Translate, Unloq and Popin. Why it costs orders

Google's mobile research found that the chance of a bounce rises 32% as load time goes from 1s to 3s, and 90% at 5s. Diwali is on 8 November, 23 days away, and Shop All is already set up for gifting. Every ad rupee spent during the festive peak lands on these pages.

What we will change, with TrooPerformance
  • Fix the three templates in under 48 hours on a duplicate theme, starting with the bestseller page, then Shop All and the homepage. We load the product image first, defer render-blocking files and set the load order for third-party apps. Nothing goes live until the Bombae team has reviewed it.
  • Monthly audit: we re-run the audit every month, flag any change and work to hold the score we delivered.
  • Change guidelines: a living guide for Bombae's tech or dev agency, so future changes don't quietly slow the site down again.
  • Plugin coordination: we work directly with SagePilot, GoKwik and the other app vendors to optimise their scripts. This needs introductions from the Bombae team.

  • The llms.txt and AGENTS.md files that AI agents read first are Shopify's default text and don't name any Bombae product.

The plan

Three phases over eight weeks, starting with the buy path

Phase 1 fixes confirmed bugs in the shared theme, cart and checkout, so every product page benefits at once. Phases 2 and 3 run as A/B tests on the two hero PDPs first, then roll out to the rest of the catalogue. Hairess and Fuzzoff together get about 15,000 product views a day, enough for a clear result in 2–3 weeks per test.

Weeks 1–2

Unblock the buy path, store-wide

  • B1: open the cart drawer on Add to Cart, and hide scroll-to-top
  • B3: Checkout button for shoppers with the item in cart
  • B5: EMI modal buy button and live dates
  • B6: one warranty number per product, matching announcement bars
  • Clarity tracking on every PDP

Then A/B test: cart drawer vs Buy Now.

Weeks 3–6

Rebuild hero PDPs, then roll out

  • B6: trust row and delivery date
  • B7: review carousel under Add to Cart, trimmed banners
  • B8: suitability chips and open first-timer answers
  • B9: demo loop on slide 2, icon USPs, review photo strip

A/B test on Hairess and Fuzzoff, then apply the winning layout to every PDP.

Weeks 3–8, alongside

Speed, cart and checkout

  • B2: GoKwik funnel audit, then prepaid offer and COD tests
  • B4: cart drawer clean-up and reward progress bar
  • B10: TrooPerformance fixes three templates in 48 hrs, then monthly audits (separate charges apply)

A/B tests: cart drawer variant, prepaid offer.
